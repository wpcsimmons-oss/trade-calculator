How values are calculated
  1. Usage, not box scores. Each player’s share of his team’s targets, air yards, carries, red-zone and goal-line touches, plus snap share, is averaged with extra weight on the last 3–4 games.
  2. Luck is removed. Yards per touch and catch rate are pulled toward the league average for the position, and touchdowns come mostly from where the touches happen (a goal-line carry is worth far more than a midfield one), not from how many TDs he happened to score.
  3. Schedule. Every remaining game is adjusted for how many fantasy points that defense allows to the position. Bye weeks count as zero. Injured players are discounted.
  4. Value over replacement. Your league size and lineup decide how good a “free” waiver player is at each position. A player’s value is his rest-of-season points above that level, with fantasy playoff weeks (15–17) counted extra.
  5. Trades are scored by rebuilding your best starting lineup for every remaining week before and after the deal — not by adding up player values. If you receive more players than you give, the player you’d have to drop is charged to you. The other team is estimated as a league-average roster.
  6. Keeper mode adds a next-season value: current per-game production × an age curve for the position × how secure the player’s role is. That is ranked into an implied draft round and compared with the keeper cost.

Every weight lives in one file in the repository: pipeline/config.py.
